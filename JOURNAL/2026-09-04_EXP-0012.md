# Journal — 2026-09-04 / EXP-0012

## Scope

EXP-0012 tested whether a bounded internal significance / motivational-affective loop improves adaptation after a rule change. The work was performed only in `--AGI-Lab`.

`--AGI` and Space were not modified.

## Execution

- Deterministic discrete environment.
- A reactive baseline, B memory baseline, C organism candidate.
- 20 independent seeds (`0..19`).
- Automated tests: passed.
- Primary run: executed.
- Affect ablation: executed.

## Result

A: no adaptation after the rule change under the defined criterion; reward mean `-40.00`.

B: adaptation mean `15.5` steps; reward mean `142.60`; switch error `0.218`; transfer `0.985`.

C: adaptation mean `14.8` steps; reward mean `115.20`; switch error `0.270`; transfer `0.980`.

Affect ablation C_on vs C_off:

- adaptation: `-0.700` steps;
- reward: `-27.400`;
- switch error: `+0.051875`;
- transfer: `-0.005`;
- C_off trace equals B trace: `1.000`.

## Classification

**FAILURE for the tested hypothesis.**

The affective mechanism was causally active, but the tested implementation did not improve overall performance. It traded a small adaptation-speed gain for lower cumulative utility and higher switch error, with no transfer benefit.

## Interpretation boundary

This does not establish that motivation or affective state is useless in general. It falsifies the tested implementation as a demonstrated improvement under this protocol.

## Architectural decision

No changes to canonical `--AGI` or Space.

No promotion of experimental code.

Evidence is retained; temporary experimental organism and execution machinery are disposable.
